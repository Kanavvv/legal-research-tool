## 2. Setup (you do this, about 10 minutes)

1. Telegram app → pencil icon (mobile) or the menu → **New Channel**.
2. Name and description from above. Upload the icon.
3. Channel type: **Public**. Link: `t.me/asknyaya` (or the next free handle).
4. Skip adding subscribers for now. Post the welcome post (Day 0 below) first so the channel isn't empty when people arrive.
5. Channel settings (tap the name → Edit):
   - **Reactions:** on, limited to a few (👍 🔥 🤯 🙏). Reactions give quiet members a way to respond.
   - **Sign messages:** off (the channel speaks as Nyaya).
   - **Restrict saving content:** **off**. You want people to forward quizzes to their class groups.
   - **Discussion (comments):** optional. If you turn it on, create a linked group "Nyaya · Discussion", pin the rule "No questions about your own case. Ask asknyaya.in or contact your DLSA", and moderate it daily. If you can't moderate daily, leave comments off for the first month.
6. Pin the welcome post.
7. Add the channel link to the other profiles' bios and to `brand/instagram-kit.md` / `brand/linkedin.md` link lists.

**Posting a quiz:** paperclip → **Poll** → turn on **Quiz mode** → type the options → tap the correct one → fill in **Explanation**. Polls in channels are always anonymous, so nobody's wrong answer is public, which helps get votes.
Limits: question 300 characters, option 100, explanation 200. Every quiz below fits.

**Scheduling without a bot:** write the post, then long-press the send button (mobile) or right-click it (desktop) → **Schedule message**. You can queue a whole week on Sunday night. If your app version doesn't offer scheduling for polls, schedule the text posts and post the poll live.

### Day 7 · Sun 22 Nov

**11:00 am · #Landmark**
```
#Landmark · Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

Citation: (1973) 4 SCC 225 · decided 24 April 1973
Bench: 13 judges, the largest the Supreme Court has ever sat. Majority 7:6.

Facts: A Kerala math head challenged state land reform laws. The case grew into a question about the Constitution itself: how far can Parliament amend it under Article 368?

Issue: Is Parliament's amending power unlimited?

Held: Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, including fundamental rights, but it cannot destroy or damage its basic structure.

Why it matters:
• Partly overruled Golaknath (1967), which had said fundamental rights could not be amended at all.
• Created the basic structure doctrine. The judges gave examples (supremacy of the Constitution, democracy, secularism, separation of powers, federalism), not a closed list.
• Applied later in Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975) and Minerva Mills (1980).

Exam line: "Amend, yes; abrogate, no."

More case briefs and moot practice: https://asknyaya.in/students?utm_source=telegram&utm_medium=channel
```

### Day 14 · Sun 29 Nov

**11:00 am · #Landmark**
```
#Landmark · Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)

Citation: (2014) 8 SCC 273 · decided 2 July 2014
Bench: Chandramauli Kr. Prasad and Pinaki Chandra Ghose, JJ.

Facts: A husband accused under IPC s.498A and the Dowry Prohibition Act sought anticipatory bail. The Court used the case to address routine arrests.

Issue: Can police arrest automatically for offences punishable with up to 7 years?

Held: No. For such offences, police must be satisfied that arrest is necessary on the grounds in CrPC s.41 and record their reasons. Magistrates must check those reasons before authorising detention.

The directions:
• Police to use a checklist of the s.41(1)(b)(ii) grounds and send it, with reasons, to the Magistrate.
• Where arrest isn't needed, serve a notice of appearance (CrPC s.41A) within 2 weeks of the case being registered.
• Police who don't comply face departmental action and contempt; Magistrates who authorise detention mechanically face departmental action.

Where it lives now: the arrest power and the notice of appearance are in BNSS s.35. The Court reaffirmed these directions in Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI (2022).

More case briefs and moot practice: https://asknyaya.in/students?utm_source=telegram&utm_medium=channel
```

## 6. Growing the channel

Honest expectation: law Telegram channels grow by being forwarded, one class group at a time. There's no shortcut worth taking.

**Law college groups.** Most law colleges have batch Telegram or WhatsApp groups. Ask a friend in each batch, or the class representative, whether you can share a single quiz poll (not the channel link alone). A quiz is useful on its own, and the channel name is on it. Always ask the group admin first; never join a group just to drop links.

**Moot court and quiz societies.** Email or DM the moot court committee or the law quiz society at a few colleges. Offer: "We post a free daily BNS/BNSS/BSA quiz with explanations. Happy for your members to forward it, or to set one week's questions with credit to your society." Guest-set weeks give them something to share and give you content.

**Judiciary aspirants.** Judiciary prep groups care about bare-act accuracy. The #OldToNew posts are the hook. Forward a week's posts together as one "IPC → BNS: 6 changes this week" round-up.

**CLAT PG aspirants.** CLAT PG tests substantive and procedural law, so the BNS/BNSS posts apply directly. CLAT UG is mostly legal reasoning from given principles; for those aspirants, point to the Constitution and landmark posts, not section numbers.

**Cross-links from Nyaya.** Suggestions for the site, for you or whoever works on the app; this kit doesn't change any code:
- A "Daily quiz on Telegram" link on /students, next to the quiz.
- The channel link in the weekly Sunday digest email footer.
- A line under the /convert result: "Get one of these every morning on Telegram."

**Cross-links from other Nyaya channels.** One line in the Instagram, LinkedIn and X bios or link lists: "Daily law quiz: t.me/asknyaya". Mention it once in a LinkedIn post after the first week.

**Inside Telegram.**
- Telegram search reads channel names, which is why the name has "BNS BNSS BSA Daily Quiz".
- Swap shout-outs with other law student channels of a similar size (one post each, no payment).
- After 2–3 weeks, a "Quiz of the week" recap post (the week's 7 questions as one list, answers hidden as spoilers) gives new members a way to catch up.

**What to measure (weekly).** Subscribers, average views per post, quiz votes per poll, and clicks with `utm_source=telegram` in the site analytics. Keep whichever post type gets the most forwards; cut whatever doesn't.

## 7. Automation: the posting bot

Built 13 Nov 2026, not switched on yet. It posts only what's in `brand/telegram-queue.json`, at fixed IST times. It never replies, never reads messages, and never writes posts itself.

**Files**
- `brand/telegram-queue.json`: the queue. The welcome post plus all 14 days (32 scheduled posts), generated from section 5 of this file. To change a post, edit it here before its date. To skip one, delete it.
- `scripts/telegram_post.py`: sends the posts due now through the Telegram Bot API. Standard library only. `--dry-run` prints without sending.
- `.github/workflows/telegram.yml`: GitHub Actions runs the script at 9:00 (11:00 on Sunday), 10:00 Sat, 18:00 Fri and 19:30 IST. GitHub sometimes starts runs a few minutes late.

**Switching it on (you, about 5 minutes)**
1. In Telegram, message **@BotFather** → `/newbot` → name it "Nyaya Poster", username e.g. `asknyaya_poster_bot`. It replies with a token. Don't paste the token anywhere except step 3.
2. Channel → Administrators → Add Admin → search the bot → turn on **Post messages** only.
3. GitHub → the repo → Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions:
   - **Secrets** tab → New secret `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` = the token.
   - **Variables** tab → New variable `TELEGRAM_CHANNEL` = your channel handle with the @ (only needed if it isn't `@asknyaya`).
